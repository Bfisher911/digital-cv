Teaching

Dr. Fisher teaches applied technology and emergency management through practical work, ethical framing, and the pressure-tested habits that make tools useful.

His teaching begins with use. Students and faculty need enough conceptual structure to understand what a system is doing, enough practical fluency to work with it, and enough skepticism to know when the output needs review. That balance appears across AI, UI/UX, enterprise platforms, emergency management, GIS, and remote sensing.

The paramedic background matters here. It shows up in the way courses move from explanation to scenario, from scenario to decision, and from decision to consequence. The classroom is calm, but the habits are built for less tidy rooms.

Tulane SoPA Information Technology

  • AI in Modern Society, graduate and undergraduate, examines AI systems as social, technical, and institutional forces.
  • AI in Action teaches applied AI workflows, review habits, and practical implementation.
  • Applied Artificial Intelligence focuses on adoption, workflow design, and responsible use.
  • The Ethics of Technology Through Science Fiction, undergraduate, uses speculative fiction to examine power, design, and responsibility.
  • UI/UX Design Fundamentals, undergraduate, introduces research, usability, accessibility, and interface decisions.
  • Enterprise Applications Architecture examines platforms, integration, governance, and institutional systems.

Emergency Management

  • Health and Medical Issues in Emergency Management, graduate and undergraduate, connects public health, medical operations, crisis response, and emergency planning.
  • Emergency Management Training and Exercises, undergraduate, covers readiness, training design, exercise structure, and applied evaluation.

Science and Engineering

  • Geospatial Science and GIS Applications covers spatial thinking, geospatial methods, and applied analysis.
  • River and Stream Restoration, graduate, co-professor. Module: The Use of Spatial Data in Restoration Planning.
